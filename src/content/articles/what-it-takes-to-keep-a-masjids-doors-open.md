When a masjid opens, there are photographs. A ribbon, a first adhan, rows of people on new carpet. Then the photographs stop, and the real work begins.

A masjid is a building that is used five times a day, every day, by anyone who walks in. Few buildings are asked to do as much. Here is what stands behind an open door.

## The building itself

Roofs leak. Wiring ages. Carpets wear thin in the first rows before anywhere else. In hot climates, fans and cooling fail; in cold ones, heating does. Wudu areas need constant care because they are wet all day. Left alone, small problems become the kind that close a building.

## The running costs

Electricity and water come every month, whether the collection box is full or not. So do cleaning supplies, and repairs to the sound system that carries the adhan.

## The people

A masjid needs an imam to lead the prayer and a teacher for the children. In many communities these are the same person, supported by a modest salary that the congregation struggles to raise. When that support stops, the classes stop first.

## The community around it

In many places the masjid is also where food is shared in Ramadan, where a family in difficulty is helped, and where the dead are washed and prayed over. None of that appears on a building plan.

## Four kinds of help

This is why we describe the work of Masjid Hearts in four words, not one:

- **Build** a masjid where a community has none.
- **Restore** one that has been damaged or has fallen into disrepair.
- **Maintain** an existing place of worship so it stays safe and usable.
- **Sustain** the community it serves.

Building is the part people picture. The other three are what keep a masjid in use long after it opens.
