When the Prophet ﷺ migrated from Makkah, he stopped at Quba on the edge of Madinah and a masjid was built there. When he reached the city itself, the first thing he established was his masjid. Homes came after. A community was being formed, and it needed a centre before it needed anything else.

## More than a prayer hall

The Prophet's Masjid was where the five prayers were held. It was also where people learned the Qur'an, where those with no home were sheltered, where delegations were received and where the community was consulted on its affairs. Worship came first, and everything else a community needs grew around it.

That pattern has held ever since. Walk into a working masjid anywhere in the world and you will usually find some of the same things:

- **Prayer in congregation**, five times a day and on Fridays.
- **Learning**: children reciting, adults studying, questions being answered.
- **Care**: food shared in Ramadan, help quietly arranged for a family in difficulty, the janazah of a neighbour.
- **Belonging**: a place where a newcomer is greeted and a young person is known by name.

## What a town loses without one

The opposite is also true. Where there is no masjid, or where one has fallen into disrepair, the prayer becomes private and scattered. Children grow up without a place to learn. People who would have met five times a day stop meeting at all.

This is why the Qur'an ties the care of masajid to faith itself: "The mosques of Allah are only to be maintained by those who believe in Allah and the Last Day…" (At-Tawbah 9:18). The verse goes on to mention prayer, zakah and fearing none but Allah. Maintaining a masjid is described as something believers do, like prayer and charity.

## Keeping the heart beating

A masjid is built once, but it is kept alive every day: by the people who fill it and by the people who pay for the lights, the water, the repairs and the teacher. Both are part of the same work.
