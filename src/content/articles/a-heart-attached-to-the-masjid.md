The Prophet ﷺ described seven kinds of people whom Allah will shade on a day when there is no shade but His. One of them is easy to overlook between the just ruler and the one who gives charity in secret: **a person whose heart is attached to the masajid** (Sahih al-Bukhari 660, Sahih Muslim 1031).

The wording is worth sitting with. It does not say the person who lives in the masjid, or who built it, or who leads the prayer in it. It describes a heart. Someone who leaves after Fajr and is already thinking about Dhuhr. Someone who feels the absence when travel or illness keeps them away.

## Why the masjid, of all places

The Prophet ﷺ said, "The most beloved of places to Allah are the masajid" (Sahih Muslim 671). A masjid is the one building in a town that exists for no purpose other than remembering Allah. The Qur'an calls them houses "which Allah has ordered to be raised, and in which His name is remembered" (An-Nur 24:36).

So a heart attached to the masjid is a heart attached to what Allah loves. The building is brick and carpet. What it holds is the prayer in congregation, the Qur'an being taught to children, the neighbour you only ever see in the second row.

## What attachment looks like

For most of us it is small and regular:

- Praying in congregation when we are able to, and missing it when we are not.
- Walking there. The Prophet ﷺ taught that for the one who purifies himself at home and walks to a house of Allah for an obligatory prayer, one step removes a sin and the next raises him a degree (Sahih Muslim 666).
- Caring about its upkeep: the leaking roof, the electricity bill, the imam's salary.
- Bringing our children, so the attachment outlives us.

## Attached to masajid you will never enter

There is a wider meaning too. A believer in one country can love a masjid in another: a village prayer hall with no roof, a community saving for years to lay a foundation. You may never pray there. But the people who do will pray because someone, somewhere, cared.
